What the state requires, in order

  1. By July 1: send the superintendent a letter of intent for each child, every year. The school year runs July 1 to June 30. Starting mid-year or just moved in? Send it within 14 days of starting home instruction. §100.10(b)
  2. Within 10 business days of receiving the letter, the district sends you a copy of §100.10 and an IHIP form for each child. §100.10(c)(1)
  3. By August 15, or within four weeks of receiving the district's form, whichever is later: file the IHIP. It lists the child's name, age and grade; the syllabi, curriculum materials, textbooks or plan of instruction for each required subject; the quarterly report dates, spaced in even and logical periods; and who is providing instruction. §100.10(c)(2), (d)
  4. Within 10 business days of receiving the IHIP, or by August 31, whichever is later, the district tells you whether it complies or what's deficient. If it's deficient, send a revised IHIP within 15 days, or by September 15, whichever is later. §100.10(c)(3)–(4)
  5. Each quarter, on the dates in your IHIP, file a quarterly report: hours of instruction that quarter, the material covered in each subject, a grade or written narrative for each subject, and an explanation for any subject where less than 80% of the planned material was covered. NYSED recommends naming your annual assessment method by the end of the third quarter. §100.10(g)
  6. With the fourth quarterly report, file the annual assessment. §100.10(h)

Instruction must total at least 900 hours a year in grades 1–6 and 990 hours in grades 7–12, on the substantial equivalent of 180 days. Keep attendance records and show them to the district if asked; it's the one record the regulation requires you to keep §100.10(f). Starting partway through the year? NYSED says to schedule fewer quarterly reports, in proportion to the time left.

900 hours a year

Grades 7–8

990 hours a year. Units are totals across both grades.

  • English (2 units)
  • History and geography (2 units)
  • Science (2 units)
  • Mathematics (2 units)
  • Physical education
  • Health education
  • Art (½ unit)
  • Music (½ unit)
  • Practical arts
  • Library skills

Grades 9–12

990 hours a year. Units are totals across all four grades.

  • English (4 units)
  • Social studies (4 units: American history 1, participation in government ½, economics ½)
  • Mathematics (2 units)
  • Science (2 units)
  • Art and/or music (1 unit)
  • Health education (½ unit)
  • Physical education (2 units)
  • Electives (3 units)

At every grade, also teach patriotism and citizenship; health education on alcohol, drug and tobacco misuse; highway safety and traffic regulations, including bicycle safety; and fire and arson prevention and safety. At least once during grades 1–8, teach U.S. history, New York State history, and the Constitutions of the United States and New York State. In grades 1–6, add bilingual education and/or English as a second language where the need is indicated. A unit is 6,480 minutes of instruction in a school year. §100.10(e)

Annual assessment: narrative or test?

GradesWhat you can file
1–3A written narrative or standardized test results, any year.
4–8Standardized test results at least every other year. A written narrative may be used no more often than every other school year.
9–12Standardized test results every year.
  • Which tests: the Iowa Test of Basic Skills, California Achievement Test, Stanford Achievement Test, Comprehensive Test of Basic Skills or Metropolitan Achievement Test; a State test that measures individual achievement; or another test NYSED approves. Your district must order a listed test if you ask, at your cost. §100.10(h)(1)(i), (iv)
  • Who gives the test: the public school's staff; a nonpublic school's staff; or, at home or elsewhere, a New York State certified teacher or another qualified person the superintendent has consented to. NYSED's Q&A confirms that can be a parent. §100.10(h)(1)(ii)
  • Passing score: a composite score above the 33rd percentile on national norms, or one academic year of growth compared with a test taken during or after the prior school year. Subtest scores don't count against you. §100.10(h)(1)(v)
  • Who writes a narrative: a New York State certified teacher, a home instruction peer review panel, or another person (including a parent) chosen with the superintendent's consent, who has interviewed the child and reviewed a portfolio of the child's work. It certifies whether the child made adequate academic progress. §100.10(h)(2)(iii)
  • If it falls short: the program is placed on probation for up to two school years, with a remediation plan the district reviews. §100.10(i)

How the PDFs are named
